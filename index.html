Featured Project

Axial Impact Crashworthiness Analysis of Steel Automotive Crash Box

Numerical investigation of the effect of circular hole triggers on the deformation behaviour and crashworthiness performance of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact using LS-DYNA.

Problem

An unmodified crash box may experience high initial crushing forces and undesirable deformation modes. Geometric triggers can be introduced to initiate and control the folding behaviour.

Objective

To investigate the effect of circular hole triggers on the crashworthiness and deformation behaviour of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact.

FE Model Development

  • Crash box FE model
  • Axial impact simulation setup
  • Mapped / O-grid mesh around trigger region
  • Fixed bottom edge

Explicit Simulation

The crashworthiness analysis was performed using the LS-DYNA explicit dynamic solver to investigate the deformation response during axial impact.

The crash-box configurations were evaluated using the crushing force–displacement response and energy-based crashworthiness metrics.

Key Findings

Hole triggers reduced the initial peak crushing force and promoted more controlled, progressive deformation.
Intermediate trigger sizes showed a favourable balance between energy absorption and crushing behaviour.
Larger trigger sizes showed increased structural weakening and less stable deformation.
