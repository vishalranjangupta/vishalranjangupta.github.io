Axial Impact Crashworthiness Analysis of Steel Automotive Crash Box

Numerical investigation of the effect of circular hole triggers on the deformation behaviour and crashworthiness performance of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact using LS-DYNA.

The Problem

During a vehicle crash, the crash box is designed to absorb impact energy through controlled deformation while limiting the peak force transmitted to the vehicle structure. However, an unmodified crash box may experience high initial crushing forces and undesirable deformation modes, affecting its energy absorption performance. Geometric triggers can be introduced to initiate and control the folding behaviour, but their design must be carefully evaluated to achieve stable deformation and effective energy absorption. Therefore, numerical simulation is used to investigate the influence of circular hole triggers on the crashworthiness and deformation behaviour of the crash box under axial impact.

Objective

To investigate the effect of circular hole triggers on the crashworthiness and deformation behaviour of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact.

FE Model Development

The crash box was developed as a finite element model for axial impact analysis. The model incorporated a structured shell mesh, appropriate boundary conditions, and local mesh refinement around the trigger region.

  • Crash box FE model
  • Axial impact simulation setup
  • Mapped / O-grid mesh around trigger region
  • Fixed bottom edge

Explicit Simulation

The crashworthiness analysis was performed using the LS-DYNA explicit dynamic solver to investigate the deformation response of the crash box during axial impact.

Simulation Approach

  • Explicit dynamic analysis
  • Axial impact loading
  • Contact-based interaction
  • Progressive crushing response

Response Evaluated

  • Deformation behaviour
  • Crushing force response
  • Energy absorption
  • Crashworthiness performance

Trigger Configurations

Circular hole triggers of different diameters were introduced to investigate their influence on the initiation and progression of crushing. The figure combines the crash-box configurations before impact with their corresponding deformation behaviour after impact.

Crashworthiness Evaluation

The crash-box configurations were evaluated using the crushing force-displacement response and energy-based crashworthiness measures.

IE Internal Energy
SEA Specific Energy Absorption
Fmax Peak Crushing Force
MCF Mean Crushing Force
CFE Crushing Force Efficiency

Key Findings

Hole triggers reduced the initial peak crushing force and promoted more controlled, progressive deformation.
Intermediate trigger sizes showed a favourable balance between energy absorption and crushing behaviour.
Larger trigger sizes showed increased structural weakening and less stable deformation.
Crashworthiness performance was assessed using internal energy, specific energy absorption, peak crushing force, mean crushing force, and crushing force efficiency.
