Axial Impact Crashworthiness Analysis of Steel Automotive Crash Box

The Problem

During a vehicle crash, the crash box is designed to absorb impact energy through controlled deformation while limiting the peak force transmitted to the vehicle structure. However, an unmodified crash box may experience high initial crushing forces and undesirable deformation modes, affecting its energy absorption performance. Geometric triggers can be introduced to initiate and control the folding behaviour, but their design must be carefully evaluated to achieve stable deformation and effective energy absorption.

Objective

To investigate the effect of circular hole triggers on the crashworthiness and deformation behaviour of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact using LS-DYNA.

FE Model Development

The crash box was developed as a finite element model for axial impact analysis using a structured shell mesh, appropriate boundary conditions, and local mesh refinement around the trigger region.

Explicit Simulation

The crashworthiness analysis was performed in LS-DYNA using an explicit dynamic formulation to capture the transient deformation and crushing response of the crash box under axial impact.

Simulation Approach

  • LS-DYNA explicit dynamic analysis
  • Axial impact loading
  • Contact interaction
  • Progressive crushing

Crashworthiness Evaluation

  • Crushing force-displacement response
  • Internal energy and energy absorption
  • Peak and mean crushing force
  • Crushing force efficiency

Trigger Configurations

Circular hole triggers of different diameters were introduced to investigate their influence on the deformation behaviour and crashworthiness response of the crash box.

Crashworthiness Evaluation

The crash-box configurations were evaluated using the crushing force-displacement response and energy-based crashworthiness metrics.

Key Findings

Hole triggers reduced the initial peak crushing force and promoted more controlled, progressive deformation.
The Ø12 mm and Ø14 mm trigger configurations showed a favourable balance between energy absorption and crushing behaviour.
Larger trigger sizes showed increased structural weakening and less stable deformation.
Crashworthiness performance was assessed using Internal Energy (IE), Specific Energy Absorption (SEA), Peak Crushing Force (Fmax), Mean Crushing Force (MCF), and Crushing Force Efficiency (CFE).
