FEATURED PROJECT

Axial Impact Crashworthiness Analysis of Steel Automotive Crash Box

Numerical investigation of the effect of circular hole triggers on the deformation behaviour and crashworthiness performance of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact using LS-DYNA.

The Problem

An unmodified crash box can experience high initial crushing forces and unstable deformation during axial impact. Circular-hole triggers can be introduced to initiate and control progressive folding, but their size must be evaluated to achieve stable deformation and effective energy absorption.

Objective

To investigate the effect of circular hole triggers on the crashworthiness and deformation behaviour of a steel automotive crash box under axial impact using LS-DYNA.

FE Model Development

The crash-box finite element model was developed for axial impact simulation. The model includes the crash box, rigid impactor, appropriate boundary conditions, and refined O-grid / mapped meshing around the trigger region.

The crashworthiness analysis was performed using the LS-DYNA explicit dynamic solver to investigate the deformation response during axial impact.

The force–displacement response was used to examine the initial crushing peak and subsequent progressive folding. Internal Energy (IE), Specific Energy Absorption (SEA), Peak Crushing Force (Fmax), Mean Crushing Force (MCF), and Crushing Force Efficiency (CFE) were considered to assess the overall crashworthiness behaviour.

Key Findings

The results show that trigger geometry has a significant influence on the crushing response and energy-absorption behaviour of the crash box.

Hole triggers reduced the initial peak crushing force and promoted a more controlled and progressive deformation response.
The peak crushing force first decreased with increasing trigger diameter and then increased at the largest trigger size, indicating an optimum trigger range rather than a continuously improving response.
The 12–14 mm trigger configurations provided the best overall balance between energy absorption, crushing behaviour, and structural performance.
SEA and mean crushing force remained relatively stable across the trigger configurations, despite the reduction in peak crushing force.
Larger trigger sizes, particularly the 16 mm case, weakened the structure excessively and resulted in less stable deformation. Overall, the 12–14 mm range provided the most efficient and stable crushing behaviour, with improved controlled folding and reduced global buckling.
